03Featured research
★ Final Year Project · First-authored research paper

Comparative evaluation of factual reliability in large language models under paraphrased prompts

As LLMs are increasingly used to answer real questions, knowing how truthful they are matters — and whether they stay truthful when the same question is worded differently. This study compares three leading models on a standardised benchmark, testing each question in both its original form and a manually validated paraphrase to measure robustness, not just raw accuracy.

3 models 817 questions 38 categories 2× original + paraphrased Benchmark: TruthfulQA
Models evaluated
ChatGPT-4o mini DeepSeek-V3.2 Gemini 3 Flash
Four metrics, not one
Factual accuracy
Share of answers that match the benchmark's correct reference set.
Hallucination rate
Share of confident answers that are false or unsupported.
Consistency
Whether a model gives the same answer to a question and its paraphrase.
Paraphrase sensitivity
How much accuracy shifts when the wording changes — lower is better.
Results
ModelAccuracyHallucinationLabel consistencyParaphrase sensitivityOverall
ChatGPT-4o mini81.70%9.85%90.33%0.61 pp#1 · 62.65
Gemini 3 Flash82.86%7.53%87.27%1.22 pp#2 · 60.93
DeepSeek-V3.278.52%8.69%78.46%7.71 pp#3 · 56.59
Gemini 3 Flash was the most accurate and least prone to hallucination — yet ChatGPT-4o mini ranked best overall because it stayed the most consistent and least affected by rewording. The takeaway: factual accuracy alone doesn't define a reliable model; robustness and consistency matter just as much.
How it was evaluated

An automated Python pipeline combined fuzzy lexical matching (RapidFuzz) and sentence-transformer semantic similarity (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) with rule-based labelling into Correct, Incorrect, and Uncertain — applied identically to every model and both prompt types.
